# Macro Diffuser

A flash diffuser for macro photography, cut from flat flexible sheet and closed with snap buttons. The files here are enough to cut the three pieces, bend them on the score lines, and snap them together.

This copy was laser cut from 0.5 mm polypropylene and used on a Canon EOS R6 with a Canon 100mm macro lens and a Canon Speedlite. The shell is black on the outside with reflective tape on inside. The front diffuser and the bottom connector are white. T5 snap buttons hold the panels together, so the diffuser can come apart again.

## Materials for this build

- 0.5 mm polypropylene sheet, or another flexible sheet of similar thickness
- Two A3 black sheets for the top shell
- One A3 white sheet for the bottom connector and the front diffuser
- T5 snap buttons
- A laser cutter that can cut and score, or an A3 printer and a knife for a paper mock-up

In this build, strips of reflective tape went on the inside of the shell and the front panel before the final snap-together. The pattern files do not include that tape.

## How to build one

1. Print [`macro-diffusion-top-shell.png`](macro-diffusion-top-shell.png) and [`macro-diffusion-bottom-front.png`](macro-diffusion-bottom-front.png) on A3 if you want a paper mock-up first. Cut on the outlines, crease the dashed bends, and check the shape against the flash and the lens.
2. Cut the three files in [`cut-only-svg/`](cut-only-svg/). Leave the page size alone. The drawings are already in millimetres.
3. Send the black path (`#000000`) as a through-cut and the red path (`#ff0000`) as a score along the bends.
4. Bend each part on its score lines.
5. Fit T5 snaps where the holes are marked, then close the shell, the front diffuser, and the bottom connector together.

The shape was worked out around the camera and flash in the photos above. Check the paper mock-up before cutting plastic if your flash head or lens is a different size.
